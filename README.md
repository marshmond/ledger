# Ledger

A personal finance and investment planning app that runs entirely in your browser. Your data is stored on your device (in the
browser's local database); there is no server and no account, and nothing is sent anywhere except an optional market-price lookup
to Twelve Data using your own key.

## What it does

**Finding your way around.** On a computer there is a sidebar with five groups: **Home** (a dashboard of cards with charts for net worth, spending, budget progress, income vs spending, and more, plus what needs attention and coming-up dates; use **Edit Home** to add, remove, and reorder cards, saved on each device), **Money** (Accounts, Transactions, Budget, Scheduled, Forecast, Reports, Rules, Import, Categories), **Invest** (Net worth, Investments, Payoff, Debt), **Plan** (Income, Tax planning, Paycheck, Projections, Retirement, Goals, Protection, Roth), and **More** (Settings, Year in review, If something happens, Tax package, Sync devices, Data & Backups). Click a group's name in the sidebar to collapse or expand it. On a phone the same five groups are a tab bar at the bottom, with each group's pages as a scrolling row at the top. The Home screen's "Coming up" card lists key dates (RSU vests, tax payment deadlines, required distributions, bills, loan payments) and can download them as a calendar file for your phone. Press **Ctrl+K** (or **/**) to search pages, accounts, holdings, loans, and transactions from anywhere, and use the **+** button to add a transaction or jump to logging a trade.

**Home & Assets (sixth module).** What the household owns that needs looking after: a house, the areas and systems in it, appliances and equipment, vehicles and anything else. It opens on an **Overview** (`#/assets`); the full tree or flat list of assets is under "All assets" (`#/assets/list`).
- **Overview:** *Needs attention* (overdue maintenance, warranties ending within 60 days, finished tasks not saved to service history, jobs saved twice, records that point at a missing asset, linked payments that need a second look), *Upcoming maintenance*, *Warranties ending soon*, *Recent service history* and *Spending on assets* for a chosen year or all time. Every figure is read from the same records the asset pages use; nothing is stored. The Today screen gets a small "Home & Assets" card (counts only, no names or amounts; hidden when there is nothing to look at).
- **Assets:** only a name is required; make, model, dates, shared contacts (with a role such as installer), specs and document references (a label and an optional web link, no files) are optional and folded away. Things sit inside other things (Home > Kitchen > Dishwasher). Serial numbers, VINs, plates and addresses are hidden until you press Show. Archive and restore; mark as sold, disposed of, replaced or stored (with a review step, open-task choices and "include everything inside" for a sold property); delete permanently (only when nothing is inside it, replaced by it, or holding history; a permanent deletion also holds on your other synced devices). Found by search; linked from tasks and Calendar events (the Link a record box); added from the Add box (shortcut `a`, for example `a Toaster in Kitchen`).
- **Maintenance and service history:** "Add maintenance" makes an ordinary task linked to the asset (a few editable generic suggestions, not manufacturer guidance); "Done, log it" completes the task and saves a service record together; work with no task can be logged; tasks finished elsewhere show as "not yet saved to service history". Each record can name a provider from your contacts, an optional cost (blank means unknown) and document references. Searchable; the Add box accepts `svc Replaced pump on Dishwasher $80 yesterday`. Permanent deletion is blocked while an asset has service history.
- **Warranties:** several per asset (provider, dates, coverage, claim notes, documents); the older single warranty date is read as one warranty until a real one is added. Warranty end dates appear on the Calendar's "Home & Assets" layer (nothing is stored); "Needs attention" lists records that point at an asset that is gone, with Re-home or remove.
- **Spending and projects:** a price or a service cost can be typed OR taken from existing Finance transactions (a whole transaction or one part of a split; transfers are never offered), never both; if a linked transaction is deleted or its split is changed the link shows Needs review and counts nothing until you pick it again. The asset page shows what was spent directly (each payment once, also across a property and everything inside it) and, separately and never added in, the Projects that involve it with their paid-so-far; a completed project can open a prefilled service form on its asset. Nothing in Finance or Projects is changed from Home & Assets. Design and per-phase detail: `docs/HOME-ASSETS-H0-PLAN-2026-11-05.md` and the H1 to H5 reports.

**Household Services (seventh module, S1 to S4 deployed, S5 built and being reviewed).** Who comes to the house, how often, and when they are next due (`#/services`). A provider is an ordinary shared Contact; each standing service is an *arrangement* ("House cleaning, every other Friday"). S4 links it to the rest of Ledger: an asset's page lists the services that look after it, a completed visit can be recorded in Home & Assets (you review and press Save), an arrangement can move to a replaced asset's replacement, Projects can be linked, and renewal and notice-by dates appear on the Calendar and Today.
- **Arrangements:** only the provider and what they do are needed. Optional: how often (with a weekday spelled out), first date and time, the property or assets it serves (linked from here only), a cost *estimate* (never counted as money spent), document links and notes (with a warning not to keep codes or passwords there). There is no field for account numbers, passwords, access codes or bank details.
- **Next visit is an ordinary Task** with a due time if you give one, so it shows in Tasks, on the Calendar and on Today. Moving that one Task never changes the arrangement's pattern; the arrangement page says "moved from ..." and, if the Task's repeat, date or count of tasks stops matching, shows exactly what differs and offers previewed fixes. Nothing is ever repaired silently.
- **Change schedule, Pause, Resume, End, Replace provider:** each shows what will happen first (including the upcoming visit task, with a warning if that task is linked elsewhere), applies everything or nothing, and has Undo. Replacing a provider keeps the old arrangement with its original provider and starts a new one with the schedule, estimate and assets.
- **Visits (S2):** on an upcoming visit, **Done, log it**, **Skip this visit** and **Cancel this visit** each finish the Task, save a permanent visit (completed, skipped, or cancelled by you or the provider) and set the next visit from the arrangement's own pattern, all or nothing, with Undo. A Task finished in Tasks or the Calendar is listed under **Finished, not yet logged** until you log it (with the date it really happened). A one-off job (a plumber) needs only provider, work and date; **Log a past visit** works on any arrangement, even an ended one. History is newest first on the arrangement, provider and overview pages; only completed visits count as "last serviced". An amount charged is for your information only: it is never a payment, never counted as spent, and never changes the status. "Book the furnace service" reminders are kept apart from visits (finishing one logs nothing). The same visit logged twice (for example on two devices) is flagged **Possible duplicate visit**, never merged or deleted. Today shows counts only. Add box: `hs visit mowed the lawn with Green Co yesterday $85`. Build record: `docs/HOUSEHOLD-SERVICES-S2-REPORT-2026-11-06.md`.
- **Overview** (`#/services`, S5): Needs attention (every exception in one list with a link to the record), Upcoming services (the next visit Task plus dates the schedule expects, moved visits flagged; **you choose how far ahead: 7, 14, 30 or 90 days, default 30**, remembered on this device; it changes this list only, never the Calendar's 12 months, and if more visits match than the first rows shown it says so and offers "Show all"), Agreements to review (next 90 days), arrangements filtered by status or provider, Recent activity (logged visits only), **Recorded spending** (a period you choose: Household Services and Home & Assets payments counted once each, with a plain-words explanation of how the figures fit together) and a few deterministic **Things to know** (spending against the previous period, the 12-month estimate kept apart from recorded money, an unusual amount, a long gap). Nothing on it is saved or changed, and Hide Amounts is respected.
- **Provider page** (`#/services/providers/<contact>`): contact details (shown only here, editable), current and past arrangements, upcoming visits, assets served, Projects where the contact is a vendor. Search finds arrangements and providers by name; the Add box destination is `hs` ("hs Lawn mowing with Green Co every 2 weeks monday at 8am"). Design: `docs/HOUSEHOLD-SERVICES-S0-PLAN-2026-11-06.md`; build record: `docs/HOUSEHOLD-SERVICES-S1-REPORT-2026-11-06.md`.

**Bills & Subscriptions (eighth module; B1, B2, B3, B3.1 and B4 deployed; B5 is the new "Household finances" overview below, built and tested locally, not yet reviewed or committed).** What you expect to pay regularly, to whom, how often and roughly how much (`#/bills`). B1 is the foundation (a record, previews and figures). **B2 adds what you actually record:** the bill (invoice) and its real due date, a provider credit, "paid outside Finance", "paid in full" with no amount, "not billed this period", reminder Tasks, contract dates, a Calendar layer, a Today card, and Upcoming and Needs-review pages. **B3 lets you confirm a real Finance payment for a billing period** (see below). Nothing is ever paid, posted, matched or created in Finance; "expected" is never "spent"; an invoice, an autopay setting and a finished Task are never proof of payment.
- **Finance payments (B3):** on a billing period choose **Link a Finance payment**, pick an existing transaction (or one part of a split), read what will happen, and confirm. Ledger keeps a *reference* to the transaction, never a copy: the amount, date and status shown are read live from Finance every time, so a payment edited in Finance changes the bill's figures by itself. Several payments can be linked to one period (a $120 bill paid as two $60 payments); a refund lowers what is recorded; the same payment cannot be linked twice to one period, and a whole transaction cannot sit beside one of its own parts. You always see **Finance's own status** beside each payment (pending, cleared, reconciled): those are your own marks and imports, so Ledger never says a payment is "settled". A bill shows **Paid**, **Partly paid ($50 of $120)**, **Payment recorded**, or **Needs review** if a linked transaction was deleted, changed in structure or reversed (the link is kept, counts for nothing until you look, and is never silently moved to another transaction). Nothing in Finance is ever created, changed, cleared or reconciled, and nothing is linked just because a transaction looks alike. A **payment history** on each bill shows every period (what was billed, the Finance payments, anything you typed as paid outside Finance, credits, what is still owed) and survives schedule changes, pausing and ending.
- **Finding the tedious part for you (B3.1, suggestions only):** **Discover recurring expenses** (on the Bills overview) looks at the payments already in Finance for payees that repeat at a steady spacing and lists them as *Strong pattern*, *Possible pattern* or *Needs more information* (two payments only; no frequency is claimed). It never lists income, transfers, loan payments or a bill you ended, counts duplicated entries once, and never shows a percentage. **Review** explains why and shows the payments it saw; **Create a bill from this suggestion** opens the normal Add form prefilled (nothing is saved until you press Add, and no past payment is linked); **Link to an existing bill**, **Not a recurring expense** and **Dismiss for now** are separate choices (a changed pattern may come back; you can take any choice back under *Set aside*). When you **Link a Finance payment** to a billing period, Ledger first shows up to three **suggested payments** with the evidence (payee, date, amount when reliable, a posting from the linked Scheduled payment) and anything uncertain; **Use this payment** only goes to the usual preview and confirmation, **Not this payment** is remembered for that billing period only, and a payment already linked elsewhere is shown separately. A bill can point at an existing **Finance Scheduled payment** (income, transfers, missing rules and a payment another bill holds are refused): the bill shows a read-only *Payment instruction in Finance* panel with a calm note where the two disagree; Finance still controls the payment, and nothing in Finance is ever created, changed, posted or skipped. A linked Finance marker and the bill's own marker on the same date no longer appear twice on the Calendar (only while the Finance marker is shown, so the approval action is never hidden).
- **One source of money per period (B3):** a payment is either typed as "paid outside Finance" or linked from Finance, never both added together. **Replace the typed payment with a Finance payment** explains the change, needs your confirmation, and sets the typed payment aside (it stays in the history, it is not erased). If two devices disagree (one typed, one linked) both are kept, the Finance payment counts, the typed amount is left out, and Needs review offers an explicit choice. Bills' own recorded-payment figure counts each Finance payment once, even if it is linked from several bills, and does not change Household Services or Home & Assets totals.
- **Add a bill or subscription with three answers:** what it is, how much (the same each time, it varies, or you do not know) and how often (weekly, every 2 or 4 weeks, every N weeks, monthly on a date, the last day or last business day, every 2, 3 or 6 months, every N months, once a year, twice a month, or no regular schedule). Who pays it (automatic, by hand, not sure), the provider (an ordinary shared Contact, reused by name, never duplicated), a type, a how-important label, the account it is expected to come from (read from Finance, never changed), document links and notes are under **More details**. The form shows the next three expected dates before you save.
- **Overview, All bills and a page per bill:** counts of active, paused and ended; **expected recurring costs for the next 12 months**, split into Known (fixed amounts), Estimated (amounts that vary) and a count of unknown ones (never counted as zero), with an "averaged per month" line that is only the 12-month total divided by 12; expected dates for the next 45 days (a preview, nothing is created); your **review list**; and things worth a look. Loans stay on the Loans page and are left out of these figures.
- **Pause, Resume, End, Reopen, Delete:** each shows what will happen first and has a guarded Undo that refuses if anything changed since. A bill with any history cannot be permanently deleted: End it instead. Ending and reopening never invent a pile of missed bills.
- **Billing periods (B2):** each bill page lists its periods (a date and a plain description such as "December 2026", never an internal code) with one label (Upcoming, Due soon, Due today, Overdue, Not confirmed paid, Scheduled for automatic payment, Paid, Partly paid, Marked paid by you, Skipped, Due date unclear) and a mandatory line saying what Ledger actually knows. **Enter the bill** (amount, actual due date, document number), **Set the due date**, **Mark paid** (paid outside Finance with an optional amount, or paid in full with no amount), **Skip this period**, **Add a credit**, **Record an extra bill**. A confirmed $0.00 bill is zero, not unknown; an unknown amount is never zero; two different amounts leave the amount owed unknown instead of picking one. Every form previews what will happen, saves once if pressed twice, and has a guarded Undo.
- **Reminders and contract dates (B2):** turn on "remind me to pay it" and Bills keeps **one** Task for the next unpaid bill (never one per future month; none for automatic payments unless you ask). The Task date is a reminder date a few days before the due date and both are shown. Finishing the Task does **not** record a payment: use **Done, mark paid** (it writes your note, finishes the Task and makes the next one together). A Task finished elsewhere shows as "finished, not yet confirmed". Renewal, cancel-by and trial-end dates get Calendar markers and, for cancel-by and trial end, a Task; nothing ever renews or cancels by itself.
- **Records that disagree (B2):** two bills, two due dates, a skip beside a payment, two typed payment amounts, look-alike records, records of a missing bill, records this version cannot read and records left behind by a schedule change are listed on **Needs review**, side by side, with three choices (Use this one, These are different bills, Leave as is). Nothing is merged or deleted: a set-aside record stays visible as history. "Move to another billing period" previews every record that moves and is undoable.
- **Calendar, Today and Upcoming (B2):** a read-only "Bills & Subscriptions" Calendar layer shows the next 12 months of expected bills (worked out on demand, nothing stored, titles never carry an amount). The Today card shows counts only. Upcoming shows the next 90 days by week, with the expected cash needed in 7, 30 and 90 days (known, estimated, unknown). That figure is not a payment and is not added to the Finance cash outlook.
- **Search** finds bills by title, provider name and type only (no amounts, notes or links); Quick Add `bs Streamly 15.49 monthly 12th autopay` (also `subscription` and `sub`; Finance keeps `b` and `bill`). Hide Amounts masks every figure.
- **Today counts (B3)** now also say how many linked payments need review or changed in Finance, how many bills are partly paid and due, how many automatic payments are not yet seen in Finance, and how many bills hold both a typed and a Finance payment or one payment linked more than once. Counts and words only: no bill name and no amount.
- **The life of a subscription (B4):** each bill page has a **Subscription life** card (collapsed for ordinary bills). It records what YOU know and never cancels, renews, converts, reprices or ends anything. **Trials:** record a trial (dates, payment method on file, converts automatically or not, price after, must-cancel-by date), extend it, and say what happened; Ledger never assumes a trial became a paid plan. **Price history:** record a price from a date (fixed, estimated or "not known", which is never treated as zero); it changes only what is expected, never a bill you recorded, a payment or Finance, and a preview says which future bills change. **Renewals:** record the renewal date, how often it repeats, notice needed and whether you must renew it yourself; Ledger shows the next ones on the Calendar (worked out, never stored) and asks "Did it renew?" when a date passes. **Cancellation:** *Record a cancellation request* (Ledger is only recording it, not contacting the provider; the subscription stays active) and, separately, *Record a cancellation confirmation* with its effective date: expected bills stop after that date, nothing is ended for you, and withdrawals, a provider changing the date and reactivation are recorded as new notes with the old ones kept in a readable History. **Checks:** possible charges after a cancellation date and a Finance scheduled payment that is still active for a cancelled bill are listed on Needs review (nothing is disputed, linked or changed); a latest payment that differs from the expected price raises a calm notice, never a change. **Disagreements** between two devices (two cancellation dates, two prices for one day, two renewal dates) are shown, never silently picked. **Household Services:** an optional link between a bill and a service arrangement; it only lets the Calendar leave out a duplicate cancel-by or renewal marker on the same day, and never hides a visit or changes a total.
- **Not built yet (later phases):** creating a Finance scheduled item from a bill (B3.2), replacing a subscription with a successor in one step.

**Household finances (B5; built and tested locally, not yet reviewed or committed).** One read-only page at `#/household` that brings Bills & Subscriptions, Household Services and Home & Assets together WITHOUT mixing up three different ideas: **what is expected soon**, **what you have committed to**, and **what has been recorded**. It owns no records and writes nothing to any module or to Finance; to fix something you open the record it links to.
- **Expected payments (7, 30 or 90 days):** the open bills coming due (what is still to pay) and the estimated service visits, split into **known**, **estimated**, **not known** and **in dispute**. A payment with no known amount is a count, never $0.00. A Finance Scheduled payment linked to a bill is counted once, as the bill; Finance's forecast is never added.
- **Commitments (next twelve months):** the same bills and services over a year, using each subscription's recorded price changes and stopping after a *confirmed* cancellation date (a request alone changes nothing; two different confirmed dates change nothing until you choose). Home & Assets keeps maintenance as Tasks with no expected cost, so it adds none.
- **A bill and a service that are linked** (the optional "bills for" link) are counted as one expense only when their totals for the period are equal to the cent; otherwise both are counted and the page says how much could be double-counted. A real extra charge (for example an extra visit) is never swallowed.
- **Recorded spending:** each payment counted once however many records point at it (a Finance payment linked from a bill, a service visit and a maintenance record is one payment), refunds subtracted, outside-Finance amounts you typed added as typed, never matched to a Finance payment by amount, date or name. Finance's own words (pending, cleared, reconciled) are shown beside the figure and never change it. The figure says **"Some spending relationships need review"** while something unresolved affects it, and **"Cross-module totals verified"** when nothing does (that compares Ledger's own records with each other, never with a bank statement; "reconciled" is reserved for Finance's statement matching). It is not all of your spending.
- **Spending by household area** adds up to the total: each payment goes to the one area whose records link it, or to *Shared between modules*. **Why this is less than the three module pages added together** lists the shared, covered and double-linked amounts. **What is counted** lists every counted payment with its date, Finance status, every record that links it and why it counts once.
- **Needs review** says what kind of thing each item is (sharing is not a duplicate): *possible duplicate*, *missing or out-of-date evidence*, *two records may describe one expense*, *subscription dates and cancellations*, and a collapsed *good to know*. **Upcoming contractual actions** lists trials ending, renewals, cancel-by deadlines and cancellation requests awaiting an answer. Nothing is repaired, ended or removed by this page.
- **Finding it:** global Search finds **Household finances** as a page (type "household finances"; the result shows no figures). **Hide amounts** (the switch on Today) hides amounts on Today, Calendar, Bills, Services, Home & Assets and Household finances, but **not** on Ledger's own pages (Accounts, Transactions, Budget, Reports, Net worth); it is for glancing at a screen, not a privacy lock.
- **On a Household Services arrangement,** a read-only **Related bill** card shows the bill you linked (its status and how it is paid) and links to it; a missing or ended bill is explained, not hidden. **Today** gets one counts-only card that says only what the Bills and Services cards cannot (possible double recording across modules, a bill and a service that may be one expense, expected payments with no known amount). Hide Amounts masks every figure on all of these; a failed read shows an error and no totals.
- **Using Ledger without a mouse, on a phone, or with assistive technology (ST-S3):** the first Tab stop on every screen is **Skip to main content**. Every page has one main area and one main heading, the browser tab names the page, and moving to another page leaves keyboard focus somewhere sensible. Boxes and buttons in rows say what they are and which record they are for (for example "Assigned amount for Groceries"). Error messages are spoken and take you to the box to fix. On a phone the small links, check boxes and tabs get a bigger tappable area (nothing under 24 px; the main buttons are 44 px); computers look the same as before. Tabs and radio buttons use the arrow keys. Deleting an account, changing the weekly-backup passphrase, stopping sync and clearing the saved sync versions each ask in a proper dialog with **Cancel** first. **Settings > General** now explains which choices follow you to other devices, which stay on one device (with or without being in a backup file) and which secrets you type again. A few text colours are slightly darker so words are easier to read. Not yet tested on a real phone or with a screen reader. Build record: `docs/LEDGER-STABILIZATION-ST-S3-REPORT-2026-11-08.md`.

**Projects (fifth module).** A renovation, a trip or any big job: a status, dates, an overall budget (or the total of its budget lines), budget lines you can add, edit, reorder and remove, and the vendors involved (shared contacts with phone and email; the same contact can be on several projects). Archive and restore instead of deleting. Projects are found by search, can be linked from tasks and events, and can be added from the Add box (shortcut p). **Tasks, milestones and events can belong to a project:** the project page lists them with progress, an overdue count and a "coming up" list; tasks and milestones are normal tasks (a milestone is a task flagged as a checkpoint, never repeating), events are normal Calendar events, and the Task and Event editors have a Project box. The Calendar shows a project marker on those items and a Projects layer with each active project's start and target dates; Today has a Projects card for active projects that need a look; each project transaction opens in Finance, and the Transactions page can be filtered by project. Spending, commitments and the dashboard are described in docs/PROJECTS-P2-REPORT-2026-11-05.md; Finance can already tag a transaction to a project and its lines (see docs/PHASE0-PLAN-2026-11-05.md).

**Calendar (second module, pick it from the top bar).** Month, Week, Work week and Agenda views of your own events (repeats, reminders, priorities, tags, people, linked records), with switchable calendars: switch each one on or off, or use **Select all** and **Deselect all** (for example Deselect all, then switch on only Personal). The choice is remembered on this device; a calendar added later appears by itself only while everything is on, and never disturbs a "Personal only" choice. A read-only **Finance** layer shows what Ledger already works out (scheduled bills, RSU vests, tax dates, RMDs, loan payments), computed on the fly, never copied; with Hide Amounts on it shows payees and dates without money figures (the .ics file you choose to export still contains them, and says so). **Tasks (third module).** Quick-add with shorthand ("pay contractor fri !high #renovation @home"), Today/Upcoming/Overdue/All and more views, lists, tags, checklists, repeating tasks, bulk actions. Dated tasks appear on the Calendar's Tasks layer, and "Add task" on Ledger accounts, scheduled items and transactions makes a task that links back. Clicking a Finance item offers "Edit in Ledger" (dates and amounts are changed there, never in the Calendar). Calendar events have a type: **Bill / scheduled payment** (can be turned into a Ledger scheduled item, which then owns it), **Finance date** (card due days, renewals, tax-prep reminders, kept as calendar events) and **Birthday** (yearly, linked to a contact). Calendar options are under Settings > Calendar. Export a calendar file (.ics) from the Export button. How other modules plug in: docs/due-provider.md. **Today (first module, the default launch screen)** is an empty frame for now (cards come next); search and the Add button work in every module, and Settings > General chooses which module the app opens to. Hooks for Today cards, search and quick-add: docs/module-hooks.md.

**Today (first module, the screen the app opens to).** One page across every module: today's events, tasks, bills and tax dates; what is overdue (last 30 days, with links to anything older); the next 7 days; Ledger's alerts (each can be dismissed or snoozed and returns when the situation changes); the lowest point of your cash forecast; and event reminders that have come due. Tasks can be ticked off in place with Undo; bills only open in Ledger. A **Hide amounts** switch masks every dollar figure, and **Edit layout** reorders, resizes or hides cards (saved on each device). **Search (Ctrl+K, / or the Search button, in every module)** now finds Ledger pages, accounts, holdings, loans, scheduled items and transactions, plus Calendar events (title, notes, location), tasks (title, notes, checklist), tags and contacts, grouped by module, and opens each at the right record; it also offers Add a task and Add an event. **Add box (the + button, or the N key when you are not typing in a box, in every module):** pick Task, Event, Transaction or Scheduled payment, or start the line with a shortcut (t, e, $, b); it shows what it understood ("Will add: ...") before saving, and a line it cannot place is never guessed. **Minimum balance:** set a minimum balance on any checking or savings account (on its own page) and Today, Home and the cash forecast warn you when you would go under it.

| Page | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Accounts** | Checking, savings, and credit cards with starting balances (an account cannot be deleted while Scheduled items or a linked loan still use it; the screen lists them and where to fix them); ledger with categories, splits, pending/cleared/reconciled; transfers (a card payment entered from either side creates the matching entry); an entry made on the wrong account can be moved to another when editing; a primary payment account that the quick add button starts on; amounts can be typed without a decimal point (1, 2, 4, 5 makes 12.45; switch in More); reconcile to a statement |
| **Transactions** | Every transaction from every account in one place: filter by account, date range, category, payee/memo, and amount (greater than, less than, between), sort by any column, edit rows, delete one or many (the delete screen first lists the Bills, Household Services, Home & Assets and Project records that point at the payment and what will happen to them; it deletes nothing until you confirm, refuses if anything changed since you looked or if a module cannot be checked, and deletes a whole selection all-or-nothing; those records are never deleted or altered, and their link then shows "payment not found, needs review"), bulk-change category/status, export the filtered list to CSV, and save filters |
| **Budget** | Envelope ("every dollar has a job") budget: Ready to Assign, rollover, overspending rules, credit-card payment lines, funding for existing card debt |
| **Income** | Salary, bonuses (entered by month), RSU vests (cash at a set price, or tied to a stock with held/sold choice; enter a grant once with its start date, how often it vests, and its length, and the app works out the vest dates for you to fill in with shares), 401(k) pre-tax/Roth, employer match, HSA/FSA/ESPP and other deductions, with annual caps applied paycheck by paycheck; federal (married filing jointly) and Massachusetts tax vs withholding |
| **Forecast** | Cash-flow forecast: your checking and savings balance day by day for up to two years, from paychecks, scheduled bills, loan payments, taxes due or refunds, one-off items, and an estimate of everyday spending; warns when cash would dip below a level you set |
| **Flow diagram** (Reports tab) | A Sankey diagram of where money came from and went: income, spending groups (expand to every category), and what was saved. Pick any period (this month through all time, or custom dates), switch categories and accounts on or off, save the setup as a named view, and view net (from deposits) or gross (from your paycheck setup: taxes and deductions, then take-home) |
| **Saved color themes** (More, Appearance) | Save your own light and dark color sets by name, switch between them, update them, and share one as a short text code you can paste into another copy of the app. Saved themes sync between devices; which one is in use stays per device |
| **Credit limits** (Accounts) | Optionally enter a credit card's limit to see credit left, the share of the limit in use, and a total across your cards |
| **Initial setup category** | A built-in category (in a "System" group) for entries that bring an account to its real current balance. They change the balance, net worth, and Ready to Assign, but are left out of income, spending, the budget, reports, the forecast, and the flow diagram (which can show them if you tick a box) |

## Putting it on your phone (GitHub Pages)

The app is a static site, so GitHub Pages can host it for free, and your phone can then install it to the home screen.

1. **Create the repository** at github.com (New repository). GitHub Pages on a free account requires the repository to be **public**.
   That is safe here: the repository holds only the program's code and public tax tables. Your data never goes in it
   (the `.gitignore` also excludes backups and CSV files, as a precaution).
2. **Push the code** from this folder, replacing `YOUR-NAME/YOUR-REPO`:

   ```bash
   git branch -M main
   git remote add origin https://github.com/YOUR-NAME/YOUR-REPO.git
   git push -u origin main
   ```

   Git will ask you to sign in the first time. Do that in the window it opens; never paste a password or token into a chat.
3. **Turn on Pages**: on GitHub open the repository, then **Settings, Pages**, and set **Source** to **GitHub Actions**.
4. Open the **Actions** tab. The "Deploy to GitHub Pages" workflow runs the tests, builds, and publishes. When it finishes, your
   address is `https://YOUR-NAME.github.io/YOUR-REPO/`.
5. **Install on your phone**: open that address in the phone's browser. On iPhone use Safari, Share, **Add to Home Screen**. On
   Android use Chrome, menu, **Install app** (or Add to Home screen).

### Important things to know about phone use

- **Each device has its own data.** Data entered on your computer does not appear on your phone, and vice versa. To move it, export an
  encrypted backup on one and restore it on the other (Backup tab). A restore replaces everything on that device.
- **Back up regularly.** Clearing browser data or uninstalling the browser deletes the data. The Accounts tab reminds you when your
  last backup is more than 30 days old. Keep your backup passphrase somewhere safe; it cannot be recovered.
- **Offline use** works after the first load. Ledger opens faster because each module's screens load the first time you open it, but it saves every screen on the device when it installs or updates, so every module still opens with no connection. If a part ever cannot load (for example a tab left open through two updates), it says so and offers **Try again** and **Reload Ledger**; nothing you saved is affected.
- **Anyone can open the published address**, but they would see an empty app: the data is only in your own browser. Lock your phone.

## Every January: update the tax tables

The Income tab calculates nothing for a year without tax tables (it does not guess). Federal, Social Security, 401(k), HSA/FSA, and
Massachusetts figures for **2026** are built in. For a new year, open **Income**, pick the year, choose **Start from 2026 tables**, and
update the numbers (amounts are in cents). Sources: IRS (inflation-adjustments release and the retirement-plan limits notice), SSA
(contribution and benefit base), Massachusetts DOR (personal income tax rates and Form 1 instructions), and Massachusetts DFML
(paid family and medical leave rates). Items marked UNVERIFIED in the notes could not be confirmed from an official source; check them.

The Roth planner has its own rules in `src/data/retirementTax.ts` (capital-gains thresholds, IRMAA tiers, ACA credit, RMD table, senior deduction). Check them each year too; they scale with your inflation assumption in later years, but the law can change.

## Price data (optional)

Investments, Prices. Create a free account at twelvedata.com, copy the API key, paste it in, and click Refresh prices. The key stays on
the device, is never included in backups, and is sent only to twelvedata.com. Without it you can type prices in by hand.
