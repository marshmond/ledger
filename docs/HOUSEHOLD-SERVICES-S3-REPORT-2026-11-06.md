## 1. Executive summary (plain English)

**Schedules.** A service can now repeat **every 2 weeks** (exactly 14 days apart, on a named weekday) or **twice a month** (two set days each month, for example the 15th and the last business day). The two are different: twice-monthly visits are not 14 days apart. The picker shows the pattern in words, the next six dates, and a plain-words reason if a pattern is not allowed, all before anything is saved. Moving one visit to another day never shifts the later ones.

**Money.** Open any visit and you can now record **how it was paid**: link the real Finance payment (the same picker and warnings Home & Assets uses), or type an amount **paid outside Finance** (cash and the like). One source at a time; switching tells you exactly what will be removed and needs a tick. A visit with nothing recorded says **No payment recorded**, never "Unpaid". A payment never changes whether a visit counts as done, and a completed visit never implies a payment. Arrangement, provider and overview pages show **what was paid**, counting each Finance payment once even if one invoice is linked on several visits, with fees for cancelled or skipped visits kept apart from payments for completed visits. A typical price (median) appears only when at least three completed visits have a clear, unshared payment, with the sample size. **Estimates** of the next 30 and 90 days are shown separately and clearly labelled. Nothing is ever written to Finance, Projects or Home & Assets.

## 4. S3-A recurrence architecture feasibility assessment (the gate)

**Question:** can "twice a month" be expressed with the Tasks repeat model?
**Finding:** No. A Tasks repeat is one rule (`freq`, `interval`, one `monthly` pattern). It cannot say "the 15th AND the last business day". `completeTask` spawns the next Task from `spawnNext(repeat)`; with no repeat nothing is spawned. Attaching a monthly repeat would be untrue and would make Tasks create wrong dates when a Task is finished outside Services.
**Decision (inside the approved boundary):** a narrow, pure **cadence-date adapter** in Services (`semimonthly.ts`), reached only through two existing choke points, `cadenceBetween`/`nextCadenceDate`/`isCadenceDate` in `model.ts` (which fall back to the existing `generateDates` for every other cadence). The twice-monthly Task has **no repeat**. The arrangement stays the source of truth; the Task stays the single next visit.
**Boundary check:** no scheduler or background engine; no pre-generated visits; no Finance scheduling change; no change to generic Tasks behaviour (`src/modules/tasks` untouched); no sync redesign; no table, migration or dependency. Not a competing recurrence engine: it only computes dates for the two day-rules; the Task lifecycle (one open Task, Done/Skip/Cancel through `logVisitFromTask`, Pause/Resume/End/Replace, Undo) is the existing code.
**The critical consequence (a Task finished outside Services):** with no repeat, Tasks spawns no next Task. Detection already exists: the arrangement shows "Nothing is scheduled" (`no-task`) and the finished Task appears under "Finished, not yet logged". New in S3: logging that Task records the visit **and** creates the next Task (once, from the schedule, `createsNext` in the plan). "Schedule the next visit" first, then logging, still gives exactly one open Task. Overlapping attempts make one visit and one Task. Tested.
**Result:** feasible without a stop condition.

## 6. Semimonthly model, business-day rules and tests

Stored additively on the cadence: `cadence.semi = { a: Slot, b: Slot }` (the `rule` is a monthly placeholder that carries only the end date). A slot is a day of the month (1 to 31, optional weekend rule none, previous or next business day), the last calendar day, or the last business day (Monday to Friday). Rules in the code and UI text: a day past a short month's end means that month's last day (30 February is 28 or 29 February; leap years follow the real calendar; 2100 is not a leap year); a fixed date stays a fixed date unless an adjustment is chosen; **no holiday calendar** (holidays are not considered; stated in the UI).
**Collision and boundary policy (my decision, please review, judgment call 3):** every month yields exactly two dates **inside that month**. A rule is refused when saved if, in any month of 2000 to 2130, (a) the two slots give the same date (for example 30th and last day, 28th and last day in a non-leap February), or (b) an adjustment would move a date out of its own month (1st with "business day before", 31st with "business day after"). The message gives a real example and the way out. So nothing is silently dropped or merged, and "twice monthly" keeps one meaning.
**Identity:** because of (a) and (b) each date belongs to exactly one (month, slot), so the date is a stable identity and is what the Task's `servicesOccurrence` marker stores.
**Tests (27 pure):** whole-year calendars for 1st and 15th, 15th and last business day (checked against an independent integer weekday calculation, no `Date`), 15th and last day over 31 years, 10th and 25th, 10th and last business day; leap and non-leap February; months ending on a weekend; weekend adjustments; refusals with examples and the accepted safe variants; a 130-year property check that every accepted rule gives two different dates inside every month; defensive reading; plain-words description; the arrangement model (validation, first date must be a schedule date, end date, `isCadenceDate`/`nextCadenceDate`/`nearestCadenceDate`, Task repeat is null, older arrangements unchanged, an unreadable rule is never read as monthly). ## 13. Spending calculation and deduplication

`servicesSpend.ts` (pure, read only): `payRecords` (fixed order), `servicesSpend` (per visit, arrangement, provider, module, optional period), built on H3's `spend()`. A piece counts once however many visits reach it; a whole transaction covers its parts; refunds lower totals; needs-review references count 0 and are listed; typed amounts are never merged with Finance pieces by value. Classes kept apart: paid for completed visits, paid as fees for cancelled or skipped visits, other (arrangement-level, or a visit needing review). A shared payment is shown as shared and **not divided**; per-record views may exceed the total and the page says so. Period: a Finance piece by its live date, a typed amount by the visit date, both ends inclusive.
**Arrangement-level payments:** a piece linked on the arrangement is refused on that arrangement's visits and the reverse (one place only); the same piece on several visits is allowed. Successor arrangements start with no payments; history and payments stay with the original provider.

## 14. Typical price and expected charge projections

**Typical price:** the **median** of reliable, unshared, completed-visit payments (even count: the mean of the two middle values, rounded to the cent). Left out and counted on screen: cancelled and skipped visits, visits with no payment, missing, changed or no-longer-spending references, shared payments, payments netting to zero or less. Needs at least **3** visits (my threshold, judgment call 6) or it says "Not enough yet". Shows the sample size and how many came from Finance or were typed.
**Expected (Estimates):** next 30 and 90 days, always labelled. Only a **per-visit** estimate on a schedule that can be projected is converted: cadence dates (including every 2 weeks and twice monthly) from the open Task's date or today, times the estimate. Per month, per season, per year and quote are **not multiplied and not prorated**; they are listed under "Not included" with the reason. On demand, no dates in the window, and paused or ended arrangements give no figure. "After each visit" projects only the next visit and says so.

### 27.1 Finding 1: payments shared by visits with different outcomes

1. **Before:** `servicesSpend` used H3's `spend()` result `byGroup`, which credits a shared piece to the group of the FIRST record in a fixed order (completed, fees, other). One payment linked on a completed visit and on a cancelled one was therefore shown, whole, as "Paid for completed visits". That is an allocation Finance does not contain, and it depended on an arbitrary ordering.
2. **Valid?** Yes. The total was right (counted once); the split into outcome lines was not supportable.
3. **Change (smallest safe, in `servicesSpend.ts` only; `spend()` and H3 untouched):** the total is now split into four mutually exclusive lines, `completed`, `fees`, `other` and **`shared`** ("Shared payments covering different kinds of visit (not allocated)"). A Finance piece goes to an outcome line only when EVERY record that reaches it has that same outcome; otherwise it goes to `shared`. "Reaches" means the same piece, or, for a whole transaction and its parts, the other side of that overlap (a whole on a completed visit and one of its parts on a cancelled visit is shared). Classification looks at the whole module (`universe`), not only the records in the view, so the same payment is `shared` on the arrangement, provider and overview pages alike. Typed amounts belong to their own record. The lines always add up to the total (`byClass` plus `mixedPieces`; the UI shows the shared line only when it is non-zero). Whole-versus-split safeguards, refund signs and once-only counting are unchanged.
4. **Tests (`corrections-spend.test.ts`, 10, all pass):** completed plus cancelled; completed plus skipped in either creation order; same-outcome sharing (three completed visits; a cancelled and a skipped visit both "fees"); a refund shared across outcomes; typed amounts beside a mixed piece; whole on completed plus part on cancelled (module view and the cancelled visit's own view); different parts on different outcomes (each to its own outcome); arrangement, provider and module views for one piece shared across two arrangements of one provider (every view reconciles); arrangement-level plus visit; **real two-device `SyncEngine`** (A links to the completed visit, B to the cancelled one: both devices show it shared, counted once). **Mutation checks:** crediting a mixed piece to one outcome: 8 tests failed; ignoring the whole/part overlap: 1; ignoring the module-wide universe: 2. ### 27.4 Finding 4: estimates after one-time rescheduling

1. **Before:** `expectedWindow` started projecting from the open Task's due date and counted only cadence dates from there. A visit moved off its usual date was therefore **lost** (a Friday visit moved to Sunday: the Sunday visit was not a cadence date, so it was not counted), and a visit finished early or in Tasks and not yet logged could be **counted again**. (Counts for a visit moved earlier or left alone were already right.)
2. **Valid?** Yes.
3. **Change (`expectedCharges.ts`, with a small `ScheduleState` read from the Tasks; the UI supplies it):** the estimate now mirrors the real scheduler. The open Task is THE next visit and is counted once, on the date it really has. Later visits are the cadence's own dates **after both that date and the date the Task stood for** (the same rule `nextAfterVisit` uses when the visit is logged), never before today, so a one-time move never shifts them and a visit moved past the next usual date passes over it. With no open Task (paused and ended arrangements are skipped entirely; or a visit finished elsewhere and not yet logged) projection starts after the last finished visit's date. An overdue open visit is not "upcoming" and is not counted (the next usual dates still are). Per-visit only; other bases still listed as not converted. Estimates never create a visit, a completion or a payment and never reach the paid totals.
4. **Tests (`corrections-estimates.test.ts`, 13, all pass):** weekly moved to another day; biweekly moved (30 and 90 day windows); semimonthly moved off its date; moved across a month boundary (30 Nov to 2 Dec); moved beyond the next usual date, **confirmed against the real code by logging the visit and reading the next Task's date**; moved earlier; window edges (exactly on the last day counts, one day later does not and nor do later visits; 90 days picks it up); overdue; paused and ended (no estimate); finished early in Tasks and not logged (semimonthly: not counted again, next usual date is); a repeating visit finished in Tasks (Tasks' copy counted once); estimates write nothing and total 0 paid; **real two-device `SyncEngine`** (A moves a visit, B finishes another arrangement elsewhere: identical estimate dates on both). **Mutation checks:** dropping the open visit from the projection: 9 failed (including two earlier tests); ignoring the occurrence it stood for: 2; ignoring both the moved date and the finished visit: 3.
