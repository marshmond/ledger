## 1. Executive summary (plain language)

You can now keep a real history of what the people who come to the house actually did. On an upcoming visit you press **Done, log it**, **Skip this visit** or **Cancel this visit** (and say whether you or the provider cancelled). One press finishes the task in Tasks, saves a permanent visit record and sets the next visit from the arrangement's own pattern, all together or not at all, with Undo. Moving one visit to another day never shifts the later ones. A task that was finished in Tasks or the Calendar shows under **Finished, not yet logged** until you choose to log it, and logging it keeps the day it really happened. A one-off job (a plumber) needs only the provider, what was done and the date. History appears newest first on the arrangement, the provider and the overview, with plain labels (Completed, Skipped, Cancelled by provider, Cancelled by household, fee charged). An amount a provider charged is for your information only: it is never a payment, never counted as money spent, and never changes whether a visit counts as done. If the same visit gets logged twice (for example on two devices) both records are kept and flagged **Possible duplicate visit**. "Book the furnace service" reminders are kept apart from real visits. Today shows counts only.

## 5. Visit data model

Module `services`, kind `visit`, in `moduleRecords` (ids `visit:<n>`). Fields: `arrangementId` (null for a one-off), `providerContactId` and `providerNameSnapshot` (the provider at the time, never rewritten), `title`, `status` (`completed | skipped | cancelled`), `cancelledBy` (`household | provider | null`), `date`, `endDate`, `notes`, `chargedCents` and `chargeKind` (`service | cancellationFee | other`), `taskId`, `occurrence` (the cadence date the Task stood for), `taskTitleSnapshot`, `refs` (document links). Visit to asset links use the shared Links (`serves`).
**Validation (every save):** title required; real dates; end not before date; completed not in the future (skipped and cancelled may be future or past); cancelled needs who cancelled and only cancelled may have it; whole non-negative cents, at most 1e11; a charge needs a valid kind and a kind needs an amount; a cancelled visit cannot be charged as service; document links http/https only; limits on text lengths.
**Forward compatibility:** fields the reader does not know (including S3's `payment`, `txnRefs`, `paidManualCents`, `assetRecordId`) are carried through reads and writes untouched (tested with planted `payment` and `futureField`). An unrecognised stored status reads as `skipped` so it can never be mistaken for service delivered. The reader never throws (missing arrangement, contact or Task is fine).
**Privacy:** no account, password, code or bank field (a test asserts the key names). Notes keep the S1 warning and nudge. Amounts show only on Services pages, honouring "Hide amounts"; never in Today, Search, Task titles or the Calendar.

## 6. Task completion integration

`logVisitFromTask` runs in one Dexie transaction over moduleRecords, links and contacts. Inside it: re-read arrangement, visit Tasks and visits; `planLog` (the same pure function the preview used) refuses a booking reminder, an already-logged Task, or an invalid visit; the stale guard compares the Task's and arrangement's `updatedAt` with what the preview saw; then `completeTask` (unchanged), re-date the Task Tasks spawned (same id, same links, new marker), write the visit, copy the arrangement's asset links. Any throw rolls everything back.
**Evidence:** injected failures (visit write; a Task write while re-dating; a link write) each leave the database byte-identical (`snapshot` equality). Three overlapping presses (Done, Skip, Cancel) produce exactly one visit and one open Task. A second press is refused ("already logged"). A change to the Task or the arrangement after the preview is refused and writes nothing. Undo reopens the Task and removes the copy and the visit. In the browser a double click on "Log it" made one visit.
`completeTask` was inspected first: for a repeating Task it spawns the next copy (carrying every field, so the old `servicesOccurrence`) inside its own nested transaction. Services reconciles that copy rather than creating a second Task.

## 7. Scheduling behaviour

`nextAfterVisit` (pure) is the only new date logic. Fixed cadence: first cadence date strictly after the larger of the occurrence just dealt with and the visit date (and not before today), found with the existing `nextCadenceDate` (`generateDates`). Repeat-after: Tasks' own `nextDueDate` from `min(visit date, today)`.
Proven: weekly Friday moved to Saturday then finished: next is the usual Friday. Every-other-Friday moved to **Sunday** across a week boundary then finished: next is the 20th, and the following ones keep the same weeks (the mutation test shows that anchoring on the moved Task gives a different date, so the risk S0 predicted is real). Early (4 Nov for a 6 Nov visit) and a fortnight-late finish give one open Task and no backlog. Repeat-after counts three months from the service day (4 Nov gives 4 Feb); a fixed quarterly cadence gives its own 6th. A skipped or cancelled visit dated in the future passes over that date. The next Task's time is the arrangement's standing time (a one-time time change does not carry over). No cadence, a paused or ended arrangement, or an ended schedule gives no next Task (a Tasks-made copy is removed in the same transaction).

## 8. Visit history

Provider page: visits where the visit's own provider is this contact, so replacing a provider leaves old visits with the old one (tested). Labels: Completed, Skipped, Cancelled by provider, Cancelled by household, "Cancellation fee charged $x" (each followed by "Information only, not a payment"), Possible duplicate visit; each chip also carries a mark and words, not colour alone. Editing changes title, outcome, date, notes, charge and document links, never the provider, arrangement, Task or occurrence; unknown fields are kept; a stale edit is refused. There is no delete.
**Finished but not logged:** `unloggedVisits` is read-only. A copy the owner already re-aligned is left alone. S1's `stale-marker` notice and Re-align fix still work.

## 9. Duplicate handling

Same device: a Task with a live visit cannot be logged again; a one-off identical to an existing one is refused; overlapping presses make one. Across devices: `duplicateGroups` (pure, order-independent, each visit in one group) groups by the same Task, the same cadence date from different Tasks, or the same arrangement, date and outcome hand-logged. One-offs are never grouped. The overview, history lines and visit page show "Possible duplicate visit" with date, outcome and title only, and links to open each record. Nothing is merged, overwritten or deleted by anything. Real `SyncEngine` tests: both devices log one Task offline (both visits survive; flagged; counted as the data says; a second press on either device is refused); A cancels while B completes (both kept, flagged, the cancelled one never counts as service); B reschedules while A logs (the later write reopens the Task, the visit survives, Done is refused with a clear message and the page says so); both Resume offline (S1 duplicate-open still detected with visits present). **Limitation:** whole-record last-writer-wins can reopen a logged Task; no resolution workflow exists (it would need removing a record).

### 20.1 Finding 1: repeat-after cadence and cancelled or skipped future visits

1. **Code before:** `nextAfterVisit` computed the repeat-after date with Tasks' `nextDueDate` from `min(visit date, today)` for every status.
2. **Valid?** Partly. For a **completed** visit the rule was already right: validation forbids a completed visit in the future, so `min(visit date, today)` is always the visit's own service date (the report's wording made it sound like "today" could be used; it cannot). For a **skipped or cancelled** visit dated in the future it was wrong in spirit: `min` picked **today**, the day the cancellation was recorded, so "3 months after each visit" became three months after the recording day. **Valid for those two statuses.**
3. **Change** (`visits.ts`, `nextAfterVisit` and the preview line in `planLog`): repeat-after now always counts from the visit's own date. Completed = the service date. Skipped or cancelled = the intended occurrence (the visit's date, past or future). Nothing was serviced, so the recording day never stands in for a service date. If that anchor plus the interval already lies in the past (a missed visit recorded late) the next one is due **today**, never earlier, so no overdue backlog. The preview says it in words: "counted from the date this visit was due (...), not from today, because nothing was serviced". The decision was not fixed conclusively by S0 (6.6 only says "the completion/service date plus the interval"), so I took the smallest conservative reading and documented it; no recurrence code was added (Tasks' `nextDueDate` is still the only after-mode calculation). Fixed cadence is unchanged. Paused or ended arrangements still give no next date.
4. **New tests** (`visits-correction.test.ts`, 10): completed earlier than its occurrence (counts from the service date, Feb 14); completed late (Mar 5; and a visit logged months after it happened keeps its own date); a future visit cancelled today (due 23 Nov gives 23 Feb, explicitly not 6 Feb); a future visit skipped today; a missed visit recorded late is due today; month, year, short-month and leap-year boundaries (15 Dec plus 1 month, 30 Nov plus 2 months, 30 Nov plus 3 months gives 28 Feb, a leap 29 Feb, yearly 31 Dec, cancelled 20 Dec plus 2 months); paused and ended for all three statuses; end to end cancel (Task re-dated, preview text) and end to end completed; logging on an ended arrangement (no next Task). Mutation: reverting to the old anchor makes 4 of them fail. **Results:** all pass.
5. **Remaining limitation:** another reading (a skip resets the cycle from today) was not chosen; if the owner prefers it, only the anchor line changes. The Tasks-native spawn is still overwritten by Services in the same transaction, so the Tasks screen never shows a different date.

### 20.2 Finding 2: one-off duplicate prevention

1. **Code before:** `logStandaloneVisit` refused ("An identical visit is already logged") any entry matching an existing Task-less visit on arrangement, provider, date, outcome, title (case-insensitive) and charge. That also applied to late visits on an arrangement.
2. **Valid?** Yes. Matching descriptions are not an identity (a provider can really come twice in a day); the guard could reject a true second visit, and it protected against a double click only by accident.
3. **Change:** an **operation id** (`operationId`) now identifies one press. The one-off and past-visit dialogs make it when they open (`useRef`); Quick Add makes it with each preview. Inside the transaction, a live visit carrying the same id is returned as `repeated: true` and nothing is written (so overlapping presses save once, with no error). The id is stored on the visit as an ordinary carried-through field (no schema change; it survives edits and backup). The descriptive match is **no longer a rejection**: the entry is saved, the result carries `similarVisitId`, the dialog's toast says a similar visit exists and that both are kept, and `duplicateGroups` shows both as a weak **look-alike** notice ("may be two real visits") on the overview, history lines and visit page. Look-alikes are not counted on Today. The strong guards are unchanged: same originating Task refused; same Task, same cadence date and same-day hand-logged arrangement visits still group as before. ### 20.4 Finding 4: Undo safety

1. **Code before:** `applyUndo` (`changes.ts`) removed every id in `created` and restored every Task in `tasks` unconditionally. Nothing compared the records' current state with what the action left. Done, Skip, Cancel, late logging, one-off visits and Quick Add all used it.
2. **Valid?** Yes, for all five cases: it could soft-delete a visit edited on another device (the edit is kept in the record but the visit disappears), remove a spawned Task another device had changed, put a Task back to "To do" after its state changed, remove links of the visit, and reverse a visit already acted upon (for example the next Task completed since). There was no version guard.
3. **Change:** `Recorder.seal(d)` (`changes.ts:57`) is called as the last step inside the same transaction by `logVisitFromTask` (`visits.ts:245`) and `logStandaloneVisit` (`visits.ts:308`). It stores in `Undo.after` the `updatedAt` of every record the action created or touched (the visit, the Task it finished, the Task Tasks made or re-dated or removed). `applyUndo` now first checks, still inside its transaction and before any write (`changes.ts:68`), that each of those records still has exactly that `updatedAt`; if any differs or is gone it throws `UNDO_REFUSED` ("This can no longer be undone, because something it changed has been changed since (maybe on another device). Nothing was changed. Edit the records yourself if you still want to."). The visit's links are removed only when the visit record is untouched; asset links are edited only through the visit record, so a later change to them also changes the record and refuses the Undo. The dialogs show the refusal as a message (`undoer`); Quick Add shows it in a browser alert and keeps the contact. An Undo made before this guard existed (no `after`) behaves as before, and the **S1 lifecycle Undos (Pause, Resume, End, Replace, schedule fixes) were not changed** (outside this pass). No sync policy changed; `updatedAt` is the existing per-record version.
4. **New tests** (`visits-correction.test.ts`, 10, two of them in the two-device block): Undo still works untouched for Done, Skip, Cancel, a one-off and a late log; refused after the visit was edited; refused after the next Task was modified, and again after it was completed; refused after the finished Task was reopened or re-dated; refused after the visit's asset links were changed; refused for an edited one-off and for a late log whose Task copy changed (each asserts the whole database is byte-identical afterwards); an unrelated edit to another visit does not block an Undo; a legacy Undo without remembered state still works; **real two-device `SyncEngine`**: A logs, B edits the visit, after sync A's Undo is refused and both devices keep the edited visit and Tasks; A logs, B changes the next Task, A's Undo is refused and keeps B's change. Mutation: disabling the check fails 7 tests. **Results:** pass; browser: an Undo pressed after the visit was changed in the database was refused with the message and left the visit and its edit in place.
5. **Remaining limitation:** the guard relies on millisecond `updatedAt` stamps; a change in the same millisecond as the action would not be seen. Only the records the action touched are checked. S1 lifecycle Undos remain unguarded (recommend a separate small change). A refused Undo gives the owner no automatic way back: they edit the records by hand.
